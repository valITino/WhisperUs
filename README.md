# WhisperUs

**U tell. I listen.** A private room for the two of you, for the things that are easier
to write down, whisper into a phone and seal than to say across the kitchen table.

One of you steps into the booth, says something in words or out loud, says how it felt to
say it and why, and seals it in an envelope. That person alone decides when it opens:
right away, at a time, or by their own hand. The other one breaks the seal, takes it in
and answers. Nothing more, and nothing less.

It works like any app on your phone, but there is nothing to buy and no store to visit.
What you seal is locked on your phone before it goes anywhere, and the small private
cloud that carries it between your two phones cannot read it.

The app lives here: **https://valitino.github.io/WhisperUs/**

> Until the room's cloud is connected, that address shows one page that says so. See
> "For the owner of the cloud" below.

## Put it on your Android phone

1. Open the link above in **Chrome**.
2. Tap the three dots in the top right corner.
3. Tap **Install app** (on some phones it says **Add to Home screen**).

From then on WhisperUs has its own icon and opens like every other app. Do this on both
phones. You find the same offer in the app under **Us, This device**.

**It opens without a connection.** Once the app has been opened one time, it starts in the
train tunnel too. What you do there is saved on the phone and sent later.

**A new version.** When the app has been improved, a small note says so, with a **Reload**
button. Nothing reloads by itself while you are in the middle of something.

## Open the room and invite your partner

Only one of you does the first part. Whoever opens the room is its **admin**: that person
invites the other one and can let them back in if a PIN is ever forgotten. Being the
admin opens no envelope. What is sealed stays sealed for both of you alike.

1. Open the app and tap **Start from scratch**.
2. Type your name, pick a sign and a colour, and give the room a name if you like.
3. Write a status line if you feel like it ("Slow today, please").
4. Choose a **PIN** with six digits and tap **Open the room**.
5. The app now shows an invitation: a QR code, a link and a short code. Show the QR code
   to your partner, or send the link.

Your partner opens the link (or taps **I have a code** and types the code), goes through
the same steps and taps **Join the room**. A room holds two people and no more.

## The booth

The wax seal in the middle of the bar leads into the booth. Three steps, and nothing
leaves your phone before the last tap.

1. **Say it your way.** Write it, or record a voice note, or both. If the first line is
   the hard part, borrow one ("What I did not say in the moment:"). Your words stay on the
   phone for a day if you stop halfway. A recording is never kept.
2. **How was it to say that?** Only if you like: how hot it is for you, how much courage
   it took, **and why**. Tap the reasons that fit ("It turned me on", "Afraid you will see
   me differently"), add your own words, write a sentence. There is no good and bad here,
   only what was easy to feel and what was harder, and both can be true at once. This is
   the part the two of you can work from.
3. **Seal it.**
   - **The outside:** a line your partner can read while it is sealed ("About Saturday"),
     or nothing at all.
   - **What do you hope for?** *Just take it in* (no answer needed), *Tell me what it did
     to you*, or *Let us talk about it*.
   - **When does it open?** *When I say* (it stays on your phone until you open it),
     *At a time* (tonight at 22:00), or *Right away*.
   - **What opens?** Each part by itself: how it felt, your words, your voice. You can
     open the feelings today and the voice next week, or never.
   - **How long does it stay?** *It stays*, or *One opening*: your partner reads or hears
     it once, and then it is gone from their phone and from the cloud. Yours stays.

## An envelope

**For you.** A sealed envelope shows its line and when it opens. When it is open for you,
the room shows a number and the envelope says **Break the seal**. After that you see what
your partner hoped for, and you can answer with one tap ("I am here", "So hot", "Tell me
more"), with how it landed, and with your own why.

**From you.** Your envelope's page shows what is inside and where each part is: still with
you, waiting for its time, open, or opened once and gone. **Open it for …** opens parts by
hand. **Take it back** makes it disappear for both of you, and the cloud forgets it. What
has been read cannot be unread.

**No read receipts.** The app never tells you when your envelope was opened. Nobody should
have to watch a clock, on either side.

## What the app promises, and what it cannot

- **Sealed means sealed.** Words, feelings and voice are locked on your phone with a key
  of their own before they are sent. The cloud stores what it cannot read.
- **"When I say" never leaves your phone.** Until you open it, not even the locked version
  is anywhere else. That also means: it exists on that one phone only. Logging that phone
  out loses it, and the app warns you before it does.
- **"At a time" is kept by the cloud.** The locked envelope goes out, and the key waits in
  the cloud until the minute comes. Your partner's phone cannot get it earlier. Whoever
  owns the cloud project could, on purpose and with effort: if that matters for an
  envelope, choose "When I say".
- **Once it is open, it is open.** From then on the key sits next to the envelope in the
  cloud, so that your partner's other devices can read it too.
- **The outside is not sealed.** The line on the envelope and the answers are readable in
  the cloud, like your names and status lines. Do not put on the outside what belongs
  inside.
- **Notifications say who and when, never what.** "Mia sealed something. It opens tonight
  at 22:00." Not the line on the envelope, not a word from inside.

## Keep curious eyes out

**App lock.** Under **Us, Privacy, App lock** a phone can ask for a six-digit code before
it shows anything: when the app is opened, and when you come back to it. Each phone has
its own code, and it is not your PIN. Five wrong tries mean a wait. If the code is
forgotten, the only way past it is to log that phone out (and back in with name and PIN).

The lock keeps out somebody who picks up your phone. It is not encryption of what is
stored on it.

## Notifications

Each phone decides for itself. Open **Us, Notifications**, tap **Turn on notifications**
and then **Allow** when Chrome asks. You then hear about three things: something was
sealed for you, an envelope opened for you, and your envelope was answered. Every kind has
its own switch.

## When something is off

**"Offline" or "changes waiting".** Carry on. Everything you do is saved on your phone and
sent by itself as soon as there is a connection again. The two phones do not have to be
online at the same time.

**Your partner does not see what you sealed.** Open **Us, Sync details**. The page says in
plain words what is going on and what to do. **Sync now** tries right away.

**"Changes the cloud refused".** Rare. Each refused change is listed on the same page by
what kind of thing it was (never by what was in it), with **Try again** and **Discard**.

**It still does not work.** On the Sync page, tap **Copy details** and send the text to
whoever set the app up. It holds the app version, the state of the sync and error codes.
It holds no names and nothing from an envelope.

**Forgotten PIN.** The admin opens **Us, People**, taps **Let … back in** and sends the
one-time code. With it you choose a new PIN.

**Lost phone.** Log in on another device with your name and PIN, then open **Us, PIN and
devices** and tap **Log out my other devices**. Envelopes you had sealed "When I say" on
the lost phone and never opened are gone with it.

**"This device is logged out" or "Your seat is paused".** Somebody logged this device out,
or the admin paused the seat. Log in again with name and PIN, or ask the admin to switch
the seat on again.

## For the owner of the cloud

This part is for the person who sets up the Supabase project that carries the room.

The repository is public so that GitHub can host the app for free. Only the code is
public. Nothing either of you writes ever goes into the repository, and the page asks
search engines to stay away.

### Connecting the cloud (once)

1. **Supabase:** create a new project (the free plan is enough) in a region near you.
2. **Database:** apply the three files in `supabase/migrations`, in order
   (`0001_room.sql`, `0002_envelopes.sql`, `0003_push.sql`), in the SQL editor or with the
   Supabase CLI. Before that, enable the extensions `pg_cron` and `pg_net` (Database,
   Extensions).
3. **Logins:** Authentication, Sign In / Providers: turn on **Allow anonymous sign-ins**.
   The app has no email or password. Each device gets its own invisible login, and people
   are recognised by their name and PIN.
4. **The app:** put the project's address and its **publishable** key into
   `.env.production` (`VITE_SUPABASE_URL`, `VITE_SUPABASE_KEY`). Both are public by
   design. Never the secret key.
5. **GitHub:** Settings, Pages, Build and deployment, Source: **GitHub Actions**. Every
   change on `main` is then published by itself.
6. **Check the rules:** paste `supabase/tests/security_test.sql` and
   `supabase/tests/seal_test.sql` into the SQL editor. Each prints a report and rolls
   itself back: every line should start with `ok`.
7. **Keep it awake:** a free project pauses after a week without activity. Run once in
   the SQL editor, with the same address and publishable key:
   `select private.keepalive_setup('https://<project>.supabase.co', '<publishable key>');`
   A GitHub workflow pings as well.

Then open the room. Decide together who does that: whoever opens it is its admin.

### Switching notifications on (once)

Notifications need a small helper in the Supabase project. Nobody has to copy any key:
`supabase/functions/push/README.md` has the steps. Until that is done, everything else
works and the Notifications page says it is not set up yet.

### The master key

If the admin forgets their PIN and has no logged-in device left, nobody in the app can
help. You can: open the Supabase dashboard, go to **SQL Editor** and run

```sql
select private.rescue_code('Mia');   -- the name of the profile to get back into
```

It answers with a one-time code that is valid for 48 hours. On the app's start screen, tap
**I have a code**, type it and choose a new PIN. This command cannot be reached from the
app. It lets a person back into their seat. It opens no sealed envelope.

### What you can see as the owner, and what you cannot

You can open the database. You see that envelopes exist, who sealed them and when, the
line on the outside, and the answers. You do not see what is inside a sealed envelope:
those are locked bytes. Keys of envelopes that wait for a time are in the `keys` table
until the time comes; reading them there would be a deliberate act. See
[DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), "The seal", for the whole picture.

**What it costs.** Nothing. GitHub Pages hosts the app and Supabase's free plan holds the
data.

## For developers

```bash
npm install
npm run dev         # http://localhost:5173, with a pretend cloud built into the dev server
npm run typecheck
npm test            # unit tests: the seal, sync, log-in and admin rules, notifications, the lock
npm run e2e         # Playwright at Android size: two phones against the pretend cloud
npm run build:test  # a production build wired to an address that leads nowhere ...
npm run e2e:pwa     # ... for the installed-app tests: offline start, update note, push, lock
npm run build       # the production build (talks to the cloud named in .env.production)
npm run build:push  # the notification function as one file for deploying
npm run icons       # regenerate the app icons
bash supabase/tests/local/run.sh   # the database rules against a local Postgres
```

Development never touches real data: `npm run dev` and all tests use a pretend cloud
(`src/data/fake`) that follows the same rules as the database in `supabase/migrations`.
[PLAN.md](PLAN.md) has the milestones, [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md) the reasons,
[CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) the rules of the house.

| Folder | What lives there |
| --- | --- |
| `src/data` | Types, local database, the seal, envelopes, outbox, sync engine, actions, Supabase adapter |

| `supabase/functions/push` | The notification function |
| `supabase/tests` | Rule tests to run in the SQL editor or locally |
| `tests/unit`, `tests/e2e`, `tests/pwa` | Unit tests, two-phone browser tests, installed-app tests |
